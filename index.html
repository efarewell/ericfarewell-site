When financial success is no longer enough

I'd made it.
But beneath the surface, I felt so incredibly lost.

Three kids. A beautiful wife. The dream house on a lake. Airplanes, motorcycles, and companies producing millions. From the outside, I had everything I thought I'd always wanted. I was exhausted, less present than I wanted to be, and still believing the next win would finally make me feel free.

What success did not solve

I wore my exhaustion like a badge of honor.

Most days I left for work before my children woke up and came home long after they were asleep. I kept track of how little I slept and how much I worked as if that made me a victor, rather than a victim of my own ego.

I truly believed I was doing the right thing for my family, my team, and my clients. Then my brother died. He was twenty-two.

His death did not change me all at once, but something in my heart knew I could not keep living the same way. I wanted to know my children as they grew. I wanted more laughter and dancing with my Bride. I wanted the mission inside the companies to continue without requiring the rest of my life as fuel.

The work I do now

I did not want to sell everything and disappear. I wanted a better way to live.

Over the next four years, I learned how to build better systems, hire people around values that were actually true, and move myself out of the middle of the companies without abandoning their missions.

I also began doing the work I had avoided in myself. Today, my work with founders still includes revenue, ownership, roles, and strategy. It also includes the nervous system, fear, old trauma, purpose, relationships, and the cost of a business that cannot move without its founder.

I have seen companies grow while the founder works less. I have seen relationships change when someone learns to slow down, tell the truth, and become present again. That is why I believe success and peace do not have to be opposites.
