When financial success is no longer enough

I'd made it.
But beneath the surface, I felt so incredibly lost.

Three children, a beautiful wife, the dream house on a lake, sports cars, motorcycles, and a life that looked like freedom. My companies produced millions. From the outside, I had everything I thought I'd always wanted. I was exhausted, less present than I wanted to be, and still believing the next win would finally make me feel free.

What success did not solve

For most of my life, I defined my value by what I could produce.

Work ethic, drive, and ambition became the way I measured myself. Every accomplishment moved the goalposts again. I was never fully satisfied, rarely present, and always pushing to grow.

In 2018, my brother James died at the age of twenty-two. Suddenly, the score I had been keeping stopped making sense.

When James died, I could not help but reconsider what was most important in my life. As my children grew, I wanted to be there for it. I wanted more laughter and dancing with my Bride. I wanted the mission of my companies to continue without requiring the rest of my life as fuel.

The work beneath the work

I was good at spinning all the plates, being needed at work, making money, and solving other people's problems.

I was the person everyone came to for answers, and I had them in spades. The more problems I solved, the more necessary I became. That felt like value to me, but it also taught the companies to depend on me.

Once I understood my part in that pattern, I could begin changing it. I built better systems, hired people around values that were actually true, and learned to transfer decision-making instead of simply transferring tasks. Over four years, I slowly extracted myself from the daily work without abandoning the missions or the people inside the companies.

Now I work with founders who bring their own versions of these problems. The work can include revenue, roles, ownership, and strategy. It can also include fear, old trauma, nervous-system patterns, purpose, marriage, family, and the reason all that success mattered in the first place.

Problems that keep coming back

  1. A task gets delegated, but every important decision still comes back to the founder
  2. The calendar fills with urgent work while the important work keeps waiting
  3. The team asks for answers because ownership and authority are still unclear
  4. Growth adds more responsibility without creating more freedom
  5. Work receives the best hours while family and life receive whatever is left
