My story

I knew how to build companies. Learning how to be present for my life took longer.

My name is Eric Farewell. I am a founder, husband, father, adventurer, coach, leader, author, and speaker. Those parts of me make more sense as one life than they ever did as a hierarchy.

01
Building

I was very good at motion.

For nearly a decade, my companies grew at roughly 300% a year. One became the eight-hundred-pound gorilla in its small, profitable niche. We produced millions in annual revenue, changed our industry, and built work I believed mattered.

I also fulfilled a lifelong dream of becoming an airshow pilot. I traveled, flew airplanes, rode motorcycles, and kept finding a larger edge. My team looked to me for answers, and I usually had them. Being needed felt a lot like being enough.

02
The cost

I wore exhaustion like a badge of honor.

I worked more than one hundred hours in some weeks. I often left before my children woke and returned after they were asleep. My marriage was at its breaking point, and I had broken my back twice while continuing to treat every limit as something I should be able to overpower.

I believed I was doing the right thing for my family, my team, and my clients. I did not understand how much of the life I had built was organized around my need to be needed.

The break in the story

Then my younger brother James died. He was twenty-two.

It was not an overnight transformation. I tried to maintain the pace and the beliefs I had before. Something in my heart also knew I could not keep living the same way or believing the same story about what made me matter.

I wanted to know my children as they grew. I wanted a deeper relationship with my Bride, with more laughter and dancing and less distance. I also wanted the mission inside the companies to continue without requiring the rest of my life as fuel.

03
Four years

I did not walk away. I learned how to stop standing in the middle of everything.

I slowly extracted myself from the companies. I learned to build better systems, hire around values that were actually true, and move authority with responsibility. I had to stop solving every problem simply because I could solve it faster.

We moved halfway across the country because the community we wanted for our family mattered enough to change the map. I spent more time with my Bride and children. I began paying closer attention to grief, fear, trauma, my nervous system, my body, and the state from which I made decisions.

04
The invitation

A friend finally asked me to teach him what I had done.

His question was direct: “You own all these companies doing so much, but you never work. Teach me how you did that.”

I did not have a neat answer. I had years of mistakes, experiments, support, hard conversations, and practices that had changed both the companies and the person leading them. I agreed to share what I had learned.

That conversation eventually became private coaching, the Royals Accelerator, the Mastermind, books, speaking, and a growing collection of tools people can use without me.

Still becoming

I do not think the story ends with getting it right.

The companies no longer require every hour I can give them. I have more room for my family, friendship, adventure, writing, and the work I now do with founders and leaders. I am still building, still learning, and still meeting the parts of myself that would rather return to motion than remain present.

I do not coach because I believe my experience makes me the authority on somebody else's life. I share what I have lived, what I see, and the questions I believe are worth asking. The decision remains with the person who has to live it.
