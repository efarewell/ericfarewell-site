Know your numbers

Most founders I know can quote last quarter's revenue to the dollar and could not tell you their own net worth inside fifty thousand. This is the sheet that closes that gap.

Forty-five minutes, one sitting, statements open. It gives you back your net worth, what your month actually nets after everything, and the order to kill your debt in, ranked by what each one costs you.

Nothing you type here leaves your browser.

These are example figures, here so you can see what the sheet does before you start.

Before you start

Six rules. They exist because a sheet of comfortable numbers is worse than no sheet at all.

  1. Balances and rates, not just payments. A payment is what you were sold. A balance at a rate is what you owe. The sheet asks for all three, because the rate decides the order you kill them in.
  2. Open the statements. Every figure comes off an actual account, not memory. The gap between what you remember and what the statement says is the point of the exercise.
  3. Price what you own at what it would sell for this month. Not what you paid. Not what it is worth to you.
  4. Price the business at what a buyer would wire you after the debt is cleared and the tax comes out. That is usually a long way from the number you say at dinner.
  5. Guessing is allowed once. Mark a guess with a G. A sheet covered in Gs is not a failure, it is the finding.
  6. Finish it in one sitting. A sheet built over four days gets edited by the part of you that wants it to look better.

Against the other order

The honest version of the argument. Ranking by rate costs the least money, and on a big balance at a high rate it is not close. Ranking by smallest balance costs more and clears accounts sooner, and for some people watching an account hit zero is the thing that keeps them going. The sheet runs both so you can see the actual price of that momentum, in dollars and in months, instead of arguing about it. Real estate loans stay out of this table on purpose. They behave differently, and including them would tell you to ignore everything else for a decade.

Four questions with no dollar signs

Answer these after the math, not before. They are what this is actually about.

  1. Which of these did you have to go look up, and which did you already know to the dollar?
  2. Which line made you flinch?
  3. Who else knows the number at the bottom of section two?
  4. What did you think it was before you added it up, and what does that gap tell you about yourself?

Everything you type stays in this browser on this device. It is never sent anywhere, it is not saved to any account, and nobody else can see it, including me. Print it blank if you would rather work it out with a pen.

The number is not the work. Saying it out loud is.

I have watched men carry a figure alone for years, certain it said something about who they are. It never survives contact with a room that will not flinch. That is the whole reason the sheet ends in questions instead of a score.
