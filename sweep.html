Private Practice

The Sweep

You are in the room and you are not in the room. Part of you is still holding a list, and that part does not get quiet just because you asked it to. So we put the list down.

Forty-five minutes, one sitting. Everything you are carrying comes out of your head and onto one page. You are not solving any of it today.

Nothing you type here leaves your browser.

Before you start

Five things. The practice does not work without the first one and the last one.

  1. Sit down and breathe first. Feet flat, hands open, three slow rounds. Then notice what your mind reaches for the second it has nothing to do. That is the doorway, and it only opens when you stop moving.
  2. One line per item. No order. No editing. No deciding whether something is too small or too big to write. If you thought it, write it.
  3. Nobody reads this. Not your wife, not your team, not me. If something belongs to nobody else, write it anyway. A page you curated is a page that does nothing.
  4. You are not solving today. The urge to start fixing is the same urge that keeps the list in your head. Set it down and keep sweeping.
  5. Let the silences run long. Your head does not empty in one pass. You will stall, look up, and then start writing again. The second wave is the part worth having.

The prompts

Twelve of them, ninety seconds each. The timer is not a stopwatch, it is permission to sit there after you think you are finished.

Not started 1:30
Sit down, breathe, and press start when you are actually ready.

Write in the page below while each prompt sits. When the last one is done, sit for two more minutes and ask yourself once more whether there is anything else. There always is.

All twelve, if you would rather work at your own pace.

    The page

    Write as it comes. Rows appear as you need them. Leave the right column alone until the sweep is finished.

    Mine What is on your mind The next move
    Items0
    Actually yours0
    With a next move0

    Two passes

    Only when the page has stopped filling.

    One. Go down the page and tick everything that is actually yours. Leave the rest. Some of these belong to someone else. Some belong to nobody. Some you have been carrying for a person who never asked you to.

    Two. Beside everything you ticked, write the very next physical thing. Not the project, the next move, small enough to do in one sitting. Make the call. Find the file. Send one text.

    The point is not productivity. Write the next move and you will find that half the list was never heavy, it was only vague.

    Two lines

    Answer these after the page is done, not before. Then carry them.

    One
    Which one has been on there the longest?
    Two
    Which one did you feel in your body when you wrote it?

    Neither of those gets solved today. Put them somewhere you will see them this week and let them work on you instead.

    Everything you type stays in this browser on this device. It is never sent anywhere, it is not saved to any account, and nobody else can see it, including me. Print it blank if you would rather do the whole thing with a pen.

    An empty head is not the point. Being here is.

    I do not run this to get more done. I run it because I cannot be present with anyone while some part of me is rehearsing a list, and neither can you. The page is just where the list goes so the rest of you can show up.
