Free tools

Five things I built for my own week first.

Each one started as something that was costing me hours or quietly not getting done. I built the fix, lived with it for a few months, and then handed it to the founders I coach. They are all free, and they all run without me.

Where to start

Take the one that matches this week.

They are numbered because they compound in that order, though nobody has to do all five. Pick by what is actually true for you right now. Each one says who should skip it, and I mean those parts.

  1. The Solo Hot Seat

    • 25 minutes
    • Pen and paper
    • No AI at all

    I started my first company at thirteen, and somewhere along the way people quit asking me real questions. This is a piece of the room I run for founders, built for one person, alone, with the phone in another room.

    • Start here when what you need is a better question rather than a faster answer.
    • Skip it if you are looking for a system. This one is just you and three rounds of questions that get harder on purpose.
    Get the session

  2. The Royals Time Audit

    • Two weeks
    • Voice-first logging
    • Weekly + quarterly review

    I kept asking founders to track their time in fifteen-minute blocks, score the importance and happiness, then look for the work that was quietly consuming their capacity. The advice was sound. The logging was a pain. I built the version I wanted them to have: talk through the last hour, review what it heard, let the patterns accumulate, and come back each week for one honest question and one useful experiment. Each quarter, it compares what changed and what quietly came back.

    • Start here if the calendar feels full and you cannot see what is worth protecting or ready to shift.
    • Skip it if you are not willing to log honestly for two weeks. The dashboard cannot help when the record is decorative.
    Start the audit

  3. The First Hour

    • One hour
    • Two prompts
    • Sets itself up

    I had AI for a year before it was worth much. What changed was an hour spent on my own week instead of on the tool. This walks through the audit, builds a memory so you stop reintroducing yourself every conversation, does one real piece of your work, and leaves a weekly check-in running that reads your calendar before asking how things went.

    • Start here if you are new to this, or if you have been poking at it for months without much to show.
    • Skip it if you already have a context file, a goals system you trust, and things running on a schedule.
    Get the hour

  4. Find Your Voice

    • About an hour
    • Uses your own writing
    • Done once

    AI drafts fast, and it drafts the same way for everyone. I spent hundreds of hours trying to teach it to sound like me before I found something that held. It builds a profile from what you have already written, and every draft after that starts from you.

    • Do this before anything goes out under your name. Posts, newsletters, client emails, a book.
    • Skip it if nothing you produce carries your name on it.
    Send me the prompt

  5. The Weekly Harvest

    • Runs weekly
    • Set up once
    • Wants the voice profile

    Somewhere in twenty hours of talking I say the truest version of something I believe, once, to one person, and then it is gone. This reads every conversation recorded that week, finds the moments worth saying again, and writes them the way I would have.

    • Do this if you are on calls constantly and the best things you say never make it past the call.
    • Skip it if you are not recorded much. There will be nothing for it to read.
    Get the system
Before you start

A few honest notes.

  • Three are prompts, one is a session, and one is a tool. The Time Audit opens and works here. The prompts go into Claude or something like it. There is no trial quietly waiting to expire.
  • Three of the five need an AI subscription. The Hot Seat does not. The Time Audit has its AI inside it, and the written logger works without connecting anything.
  • They ask real questions and they expect real answers. Vague in, vague out. The value lives in the specificity of what you tell them.
  • Nothing here sells you the next thing. If one of them is wrong for you, it says so and tells you what to do instead.

Don't want to do this alone?

All five run without me, and that is deliberate. What none of them will do is sit across from you while you work out what you are actually building. That is what the Royals Mastermind is, an ongoing room of founders who stay in each other's business long enough to be useful.
