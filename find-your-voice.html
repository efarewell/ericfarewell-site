Find Your Voice

AI drafts fast, and it drafts the same way for everyone. I spent hundreds of hours trying to teach it to sound like me before I found something that held. This is that process. It takes about an hour.

Send me the prompt

Hundreds of hours, and it still wasn’t me.

I can tell when something was written by AI, and I suspect most of us can by now. It’s the em dashes, mostly, and the sentences built on a contrast, this thing and not that thing, over and over. It’s the same rhythm every time, the same shape of paragraph, the same reach for a tidy line at the end.

The writing isn’t bad. It’s competent. It just isn’t mine, and anyone who has read me for a while would know it. That mattered more to me than I expected it to, because my writing is most of how people meet me.

So I spent hundreds of hours trying to fix it. I fed my books into it, my blog posts, everything I’d written. I edited its drafts line by line and handed the edits back. We set rules together and I asked it to follow its own rules. It didn’t work.

What finally worked was evidence. My sent emails, my quick replies, the note I wrote when I had to tell someone no. Those carry my voice whether I meant them to or not, and that is what this reads. It still isn’t perfect. It’s close enough that I’m confident sending what comes out of it, and I want to share it.

How it works

Seven phases, run by Claude, in one sitting. You answer and paste. It measures, drafts, and tests itself against you.

  1. 01

    Your writing comes first

    Fifteen to thirty real pieces. Texts and quick replies matter more than polished work, because voice shows clearest where you were not performing.

  2. 02

    Then the questions

    Thirty-three of them, one at a time. Rhythm. Formality. Humor. The words you would never use. Most take five seconds.

  3. 03

    The machine tells get named

    A standing list of the phrases that give AI away, trimmed down to the ones that are not already yours.

  4. 04

    It writes the profile

    Every rule carries its proof: a real line you wrote, or a real number from your own writing. Nothing goes in that cannot be traced.

  5. 05

    It tests itself

    Two drafts, one generic and one in your voice, shown to you unlabeled. Then you mark up the one you picked, and those marks become rules.

  6. 06

    You keep the file

    Save it once. From your next session forward, Claude writes as you.

  7. 07

    Your card

    A share card with your name, your face, and a picture of your actual sentence rhythm. Yours to post or not.
